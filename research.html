Research

My research focuses on high-performance memory devices: ferroelectric transistors that can be built on top of finished CMOS, stacked into three dimensions, and made reliable enough for real products. I combine device fabrication and electrical characterisation with modelling, from compact models down to first-principles calculations.

BEOL-compatible oxide-semiconductor FeFETs

Ferroelectric FETs built in the back end of line, using oxide-semiconductor channels that can be fabricated at low thermal budget on top of CMOS. Our twin-gate and dual-gate designs control the voltage distribution across the gate stack to break the usual trade-off between memory window and operating voltage.

Related work:
BEOL-Compatible Oxide Semiconductor Twin-Gate FeFET Achieving a Record-High Memory Window of 25.4 V and a Near-Ideal Memory Window-to-Operation Voltage Ratio of 1.88
First Demonstration of Oxide Semiconductor BEOL-Compatible Dual-Gate FeFET Achieving a Record-Low Operation Voltage of 0.5 V and a Record-High Memory Window-to-Operation Voltage Ratio of 1.62
Multi-Grain Ferroelectric Induced Drain Current Variability in BEOL-Compatible OS FeFETs

Three-dimensional ferroelectric memory

Stacking memory vertically to keep scaling density: 3D Fe-VNAND with a dual-layer oxide-semiconductor channel and a laminated ferroelectric stack, and dual-gate polarization-boosted charge-trapping flash for future 3D NAND.

Related work:
First Demonstration of 3D Fe-VNAND with Function-Separated Dual-Layer Oxide Semiconductor Channel and Laminated Ferroelectric Stack
First Demonstration of Oxide-Semiconductor Dual-Gate Polarization-Boosted Charge-Trapping Flash: Comprehensive Modeling and Experimental Validation (accepted, IEDM 2026)

Reliability of oxide-semiconductor transistors

Bias-temperature instability and device-to-device variability set the limits for real products. We design around them: a tri-layer stack that manages hydrogen to suppress positive and negative bias temperature instability, and a variability study of multi-grain ferroelectric switching.

Related work:
A Generalizable Tri-Layer Design Framework for Enhancing OSFET Reliability
Multi-Grain Ferroelectric Induced Drain Current Variability in BEOL-Compatible OS FeFETs

Computational design of nanoscale transistors

First-principles calculations and quantum-transport simulations to design devices before they are built: from tunnel FETs based on two-dimensional covalent-organic frameworks to the modelling that guides our FeFET and charge-trapping flash experiments.

Related work:
Toward Nanoscale Organic Tunnel Field-Effect Transistors with Small Subthreshold Swing and High On-State Current: A Computational Design Based on Two-Dimensional Covalent-Organic Frameworks
